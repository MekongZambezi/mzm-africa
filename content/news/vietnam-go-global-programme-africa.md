*Vietnam's new internationalisation programme aims to support thousands of businesses expanding overseas by 2030. For African markets, including Zimbabwe, the policy creates opportunities for investment, manufacturing partnerships and stronger commercial ties.*

**HANOI, VIETNAM | 4 November 2026**

Vietnam's Ministry of Industry and Trade formally announced the **Go Global Programme for 2026–2030** at the Vietnam Export Promotion Forum in Hanoi on Wednesday, marking a significant step in the country's strategy to expand the international presence of Vietnamese enterprises.

The forum, held under the theme *“Go Global: Together with Businesses to Reach the World”*, brought together government officials, business representatives, trade promotion organisations and international partners.

The programme was approved by the Prime Minister under Decision No. 626/QĐ-TTg on 6 April 2026. The 4 November announcement marks a public implementation milestone for the national initiative.

## From exporting products to building international businesses

Go Global represents an expansion of Vietnam's traditional export promotion approach.

Rather than concentrating exclusively on selling Vietnamese products abroad, the programme seeks to help domestic enterprises establish a sustained international commercial presence.

Its areas of support include overseas market development, cross-border e-commerce, participation in international supply chains, brand development and direct investment in foreign markets.

The programme also covers the establishment of overseas representative offices, subsidiaries, joint ventures, distribution operations and mergers and acquisitions.

Vietnam's Deputy Minister of Industry and Trade, Lương Hoàng Thái, described the initiative as an important change in the country's approach to international economic integration, with Vietnamese businesses encouraged to move beyond adapting to international demand towards actively developing their positions in global markets.

By 2030, Go Global aims to:

- Provide international business and investment training for at least 10,000 enterprise participations.
- Assist 1,000 businesses in developing international expansion plans.
- Provide intensive support to 100 enterprises investing overseas, including 30 pursuing mergers and acquisitions.
- Support 100 enterprises entering regional and global value chains.
- Assist 100 enterprises in cross-border e-commerce, including at least 10 achieving annual online export revenues exceeding US$10 million.

The government is also promoting implementation at provincial level. By 4 November, 18 Vietnamese localities had adopted Local Go Global action programmes.

## Africa explicitly identified as a potential market

Of particular significance for African economies is the programme's formal recognition of Africa as a potential destination for Vietnamese business expansion.

This is an important policy development. Africa is not merely an incidental destination for Vietnamese exporters. It is included in the government's framework for international business development and outward investment.

Actual investments will depend on commercial feasibility, corporate decisions, financing and the regulatory requirements of each host country.

## What the programme could mean for Zimbabwe

For Zimbabwe, Vietnam's Go Global initiative comes at a time when the country is seeking additional investment in domestic manufacturing, agricultural processing, mineral beneficiation and industrial development.

The new programme could support a broader commercial relationship in three areas.

**Agriculture and agro-processing.** Vietnamese businesses have experience in agricultural processing, manufacturing and international distribution. Opportunities could include processing facilities, agricultural technology partnerships and arrangements linking Zimbabwean producers with Vietnamese industrial buyers.

**Manufacturing and industrial investment.** Zimbabwe's industrial development priorities could provide opportunities for Vietnamese manufacturers interested in local production, joint ventures or regional distribution. Projects would need to demonstrate sufficient demand, reliable infrastructure and competitive operating costs.

**Minerals and industrial raw materials.** Vietnam's policy explicitly recognises overseas investment in mineral resources as a potential means of supporting domestic industrial supply chains. In Zimbabwe, commercially sustainable arrangements would also need to address national beneficiation requirements, ownership rules, licensing and local value addition.

These are areas for commercial evaluation rather than announced projects.

## Connecting policy with commercially viable investment

The launch also highlights an important practical requirement: international expansion depends on more than access to promising markets.

Vietnamese enterprises entering African economies must evaluate local laws, investment registration, financing, logistics, operating infrastructure, counterparties and the long-term economics of proposed projects.

For Zimbabwe, securing new investment requires credible, properly documented opportunities that can withstand investor due diligence and meet both countries' regulatory requirements.

The most promising partnerships are likely to combine Vietnamese industrial capabilities and access to international markets with Zimbabwean resources, businesses and local operating expertise.

## A framework for the next phase of cooperation

Vietnam's Go Global Programme establishes a clearer national policy framework for supporting businesses that intend to expand internationally.

The introduction of a partner network, measurable 2030 objectives and coordinated implementation through government ministries and local authorities provides an institutional basis for that expansion.

For Zimbabwe, investment agencies and private-sector partners, the next step is to identify projects that match Vietnamese corporate capabilities with commercially viable opportunities. The opportunity is to develop investment propositions that offer clear returns to Vietnamese businesses while supporting domestic production, employment, technology transfer and value addition.

**The significance of Go Global will ultimately be measured by the enterprises that establish sustainable overseas operations, the partnerships that become operational and the economic value those investments create.**
